My research lies at the meeting point of historical linguistics, philology, and digital humanities. I am especially interested in how texts move between linguistic traditions: the Buddhist canon travelling from India through Central Asia into Chinese, Tibetan, Tangut, and beyond; the Bible and other Christian writings crossing from Greek and Syriac into Armenian, Georgian, and the languages of the Caucasus; and the long Greek-Indian-Arabic-Latin chain through which scientific and philosophical works reached medieval Eurasia.

## Current dissertation

*The Buddha's Journey to the West — Barlaam and Josaphat in the Eurasian Linguistic Context.* The Buddha's life-story, after travelling east into the Buddhist canon, also travelled west: into Manichaean Middle Persian, Arabic, Georgian, Greek, and eventually Latin and the European vernaculars, becoming the saints' tale of Barlaam and Josaphat. The dissertation studies this transmission as a linguistic problem — what each translation kept, dropped, or transformed, and what these patterns reveal about the contact languages along the way.
